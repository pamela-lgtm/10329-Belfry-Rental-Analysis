02 / Our leasing philosophy

Vacancy is the silent tax.

Our goal is to secure a qualified resident and an executed lease within 21 days of a show-ready marketing launch, with the earliest practical move-in date. This is a performance target, not a guarantee. A signed lease and the first day of paid occupancy are different milestones; we track both.

A higher advertised rent only helps if the property actually earns it. We focus on rental income collected over time, rather than winning a higher asking price that is offset by weeks of vacancy.

03 / Presentation is part of the rental strategy

Show-ready sets the standard from day one.

We want a resident who values the home, pays reliably and takes pride in where they live. A clean, cared-for property gives prospective residents a reason to choose it—and communicates the care we expect throughout the tenancy.

Our philosophy is that a strong presentation, fair market pricing and responsive management help attract residents who want to make the home their own and be good stewards of it. Condition alone cannot establish a renter’s qualifications or guarantee timely payments or property care. Consistent screening, documented move-in condition, clear expectations and ongoing maintenance support that goal.

Complete before photography and showings

  • Deep-clean kitchens, bathrooms, appliances, cabinets and windows; remove odors.
  • Clean and assess carpet. Repair or replace worn, stained or odorous flooring; avoid promising that future work will resolve what a prospect sees today.
  • Touch up or repaint visibly worn walls and trim; address mismatched finishes.
  • Verify HVAC, plumbing, appliances, lights, locks and doors work properly.
  • Refresh the entry and screened patio; remove personal items and complete all agreed repairs.

Make choosing the home straightforward

  • Photograph the finished condition accurately with bright, uncluttered rooms.
  • Provide convenient showing access and prompt responses to inquiries.
  • State rent, required fees, availability, included services and application criteria clearly.
  • Apply consistent qualification standards, including verifiable income and rental history.
  • Document the move-in condition and explain resident and owner maintenance responsibilities.
For this home: the listing reports carpet, tile and laminate. The current $2,950 competitor describes hard floors in the bedrooms and upstairs. Assess the subject’s actual carpet condition before launch; do not assume a flooring upgrade is necessary or will pay for itself without a walkthrough and estimate.
04 / The first three weeks

Act on feedback while the listing is fresh.

Days 1–7

Launch only after preparation is complete. Track inquiries, showings, application activity and repeated objections. Resolve access, photography or condition issues promptly.

Days 8–14

If activity is weak or showings do not convert, review price against current alternatives. Discuss moving toward $2,900 when evidence supports it rather than waiting without a plan.

Days 15–21

Reassess competing availability, condition and price with the owner. Pursue a qualified application and executed lease; confirm the actual paid move-in date and any HOA approval timing.

Price changes require owner authorization. We do not relax screening standards to meet a marketing deadline.

05 / Before switching from sale to rental

Confirm the details before going live.

Readiness and authorization

  • Coordinate the end or withdrawal of sale marketing with the sales broker before the rental launch; avoid conflicting availability and simultaneous sale/rent messaging.
  • Confirm HOA leasing eligibility, required approvals, minimum term, fees, processing time, pet rules and resident access to amenities. The subject listing reports a 1–2 year minimum lease range; obtain the actual governing requirements.
  • Confirm which services and amenities are included. Do not imply golf membership is included.
  • Verify room layout and pool details: the listing conflicts on primary-bedroom location and lists both private and community pool fields. This analysis does not assume a private pool.

Owner budgeting

The sale listing reports HOA dues of $7,992 annually ($666/month). Confirm the current amount, assessments and covered services directly with the association.

At $2,950, scheduled annual gross rent is $35,400 before vacancy and all expenses. HOA dues alone would reduce that to $27,408 before taxes, insurance, management, leasing, repairs, financing and reserves.

Review rental insurance and property-tax treatment with your providers. The sale listing’s historical taxes and homestead notation should not be treated as a confirmed future rental-property budget.

Our recommendation

Prepare the property to show at its best, launch at $2,950 per month, and review the response weekly. Prioritize a qualified resident and dependable occupancy over holding out for a small premium that vacancy can quickly consume.
